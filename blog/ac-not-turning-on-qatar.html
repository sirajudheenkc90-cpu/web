AC Not Turning On or Tripping the Breaker in Qatar

A completely dead AC — or one that trips the breaker every time it starts — has a handful of likely causes. Here's what to check safely, and what needs an engineer.

An AC that won't start usually has a power, control or electrical-component fault. Common causes are a tripped breaker, a thermostat set wrong or with dead batteries, a failed capacitor or contactor, a safety switch (like a full condensate float) that's cut power, or a compressor problem. A breaker that trips repeatedly is a real electrical fault — never just keep resetting it.

Start with the safe checks

  • Thermostat: set to Cool, setpoint below room temperature, fresh batteries.
  • Power: check the breaker hasn't tripped and any isolator switch is on. Reset a tripped breaker once — if it trips again, stop.
  • Condensate safety switch: a full drain pan can trip a float switch and cut power by design — a blocked drain may be the real cause.
  • Outdoor unit: confirm it isn't obstructed or overheating.

Important: if the breaker trips again after one reset, stop and call a technician. A repeatedly tripping breaker means a short, an overloaded compressor or a wiring fault — resetting it repeatedly is a fire and equipment risk.

Faults that need an engineer

  • Failed run/start capacitor: a very common no-start cause — the compressor or fan can't get going.
  • Contactor failure: the relay that powers the outdoor unit sticks or burns out.
  • Compressor fault or seized motor: can draw high current and trip the breaker.
  • Wiring, control board or transformer faults.
