What Size AC Do I Need in Qatar? An Engineer's Sizing Guide

Buy an AC that's too small and it never cools; too big and it short-cycles and wastes money. Here's how AC sizing actually works in Qatar's climate — and why rules of thumb get it wrong.

Correct AC size comes from a cooling-load calculation, not a fixed "tons per square metre" rule. The right capacity depends on room area and ceiling height, glazing and orientation, insulation, occupancy, equipment heat, and — critically in Qatar — the extreme outdoor design temperature. Oversizing and undersizing both cause problems, so the goal is to match capacity to the actual load.

Why rules of thumb fail in Qatar

Generic "one ton per X square metres" guidance ignores the things that dominate the load here: 45–50°C outdoor design temperatures, large sun-facing windows, roof heat gain, and how the space is used. Two rooms of the same size can need very different capacities. That's why we calculate rather than guess.

What actually drives the size

  • Area and ceiling height (volume of air to cool).
  • Glazing: window size, orientation and shading — solar gain is huge in Qatar.
  • Insulation: roof and wall construction.
  • Occupancy and equipment: people, kitchens, IT and lighting all add heat.
  • Air changes / fresh-air needs for the space type.
  • Outdoor design temperature — the peak the system must handle.

Why size matters both ways

  • Undersized: runs constantly, never reaches setpoint, high bills, early wear.
  • Oversized: short-cycles, cools fast but doesn't dehumidify, leaving rooms cold and clammy — and wastes money on the purchase and running.

How an AGM engineer sizes your system

We carry out a room-by-room cooling-load calculation for your actual building and use, then recommend the right capacity and system type — split, ducted, or VRF for multi-room control.
